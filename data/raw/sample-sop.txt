STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE
GSE Thermal Control System
Document: SOP-TCS-001
Revision: A
Date: January 2026

1.0 PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This Standard Operating Procedure establishes the requirements and procedures for
operating, monitoring, and maintaining the Ground Support Equipment (GSE) Thermal
Control System (TCS). This SOP applies to all personnel involved in thermal management
operations during pre-launch, launch, and post-launch activities.

2.0 SAFETY REQUIREMENTS

2.1 Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

All personnel operating the Thermal Control System must wear the following safety
equipment at all times:
- Safety glasses with side shields
- Heat-resistant gloves rated to 250 degrees Fahrenheit
- Steel-toed boots
- Flame-resistant coveralls (FRC)
- Hearing protection when coolant pumps are active

2.2 Hazard Awareness

WARNING: The coolant fluid (Fluorinert FC-77) can cause skin irritation on prolonged
contact. In case of skin exposure, flush affected area with water for 15 minutes and
report to the Safety Officer.

CAUTION: System pressure must not exceed 150 PSI during normal operations. Overpressure
events require immediate shutdown per Section 6.0.

3.0 SYSTEM INITIALIZATION

3.1 Pre-Start Checklist

Before initializing the Thermal Control System, verify the following:
1. All circuit breakers are in the OFF position
2. Coolant reservoir level is between MIN and MAX marks
3. All manual valves are in the CLOSED position
4. Pressure relief valves are functional (check tags for last inspection date)
5. Emergency stop buttons are accessible and unobstructed
6. Communication with Mission Control is established

3.2 Startup Procedure

STEP 1: Engage main power breaker on Panel A-7
STEP 2: Wait 30 seconds for system self-test to complete
STEP 3: Verify green STATUS indicator on the control panel
STEP 4: Open primary coolant valve V-101 slowly (quarter-turn increments)
STEP 5: Start coolant pump P-1 using the PUMP START button
STEP 6: Monitor flow rate until it stabilizes at 15-20 GPM
STEP 7: Open secondary coolant valve V-102
STEP 8: Start coolant pump P-2
STEP 9: Verify system pressure is between 80-120 PSI
STEP 10: Set temperature controller to mission-specified setpoint

3.3 Post-Startup Verification

After startup, monitor the following parameters for 15 minutes:
- Coolant temperature: within +/- 2 degrees of setpoint
- System pressure: 80-120 PSI
- Flow rate: 15-20 GPM per loop
- Vibration levels: below 0.5 in/sec
Record all readings in the Operations Log (Form TCS-LOG-001).

4.0 NORMAL OPERATIONS

4.1 Monitoring Requirements

During normal operations, operators shall:
- Check system parameters every 30 minutes
- Record readings in the Operations Log
- Report any anomalies to the Shift Supervisor immediately
- Maintain communication with Mission Control

4.2 Parameter Limits

The following parameters must be maintained during operations:

Temperature:
- Coolant supply: Setpoint +/- 2 degrees F
- Coolant return: Setpoint + 10 to + 20 degrees F
- Ambient air: 65-85 degrees F

Pressure:
- System pressure: 80-120 PSI
- Differential pressure: 10-30 PSI

Flow:
- Primary loop: 15-20 GPM
- Secondary loop: 15-20 GPM

4.3 Shift Handover

At each shift change, the outgoing operator shall:
1. Brief the incoming operator on system status
2. Document any anomalies or pending actions
3. Transfer the Operations Log
4. Perform a joint walkdown of the system
5. Both operators sign the Shift Handover Form (TCS-SHF-001)

5.0 CALIBRATION PROCEDURES

5.1 Temperature Sensor Calibration

Temperature sensors shall be calibrated every 90 days using the following procedure:
1. Obtain a NIST-traceable reference thermometer
2. Immerse sensor and reference in a controlled temperature bath
3. Record readings at 50, 100, 150, and 200 degrees F
4. Calculate offset at each point
5. If offset exceeds +/- 0.5 degrees F, replace the sensor
6. Document results in the Calibration Log (Form TCS-CAL-001)

5.2 Pressure Gauge Calibration

Pressure gauges shall be calibrated every 180 days:
1. Connect gauge to a dead-weight pressure tester
2. Apply known pressures at 0, 50, 100, and 150 PSI
3. Record gauge readings at each point
4. If error exceeds +/- 2 PSI, replace the gauge
5. Apply calibration sticker with date and technician initials

5.3 Flow Meter Calibration

Flow meters shall be calibrated annually by the manufacturer or an accredited lab.
Submit calibration certificates to the Quality Assurance department.

6.0 EMERGENCY PROCEDURES

6.1 Emergency Shutdown

In the event of any of the following conditions, initiate an emergency shutdown:
- Coolant leak detected
- System pressure exceeds 150 PSI
- Coolant temperature exceeds setpoint by more than 20 degrees F
- Loss of electrical power
- Fire or smoke detected
- Seismic event
- Direction from Mission Control

EMERGENCY SHUTDOWN PROCEDURE:
1. Press the red EMERGENCY STOP button (any of three locations)
2. Close manual isolation valves V-101 and V-102
3. Notify Mission Control: "TCS Emergency Shutdown Initiated"
4. Notify the Shift Supervisor and Safety Officer
5. Do NOT attempt to restart without authorization from the Test Director
6. Document the event in the Incident Report (Form TCS-IR-001)

6.2 Coolant Leak Response

If a coolant leak is detected:
1. Initiate emergency shutdown per Section 6.1
2. Evacuate non-essential personnel from the area
3. Don appropriate PPE (chemical splash goggles, nitrile gloves)
4. Contain the spill using absorbent materials from the spill kit
5. Do NOT wash coolant into floor drains
6. Contact Environmental Health and Safety (EHS) at extension 4-SAFE
7. Complete Spill Report (Form EHS-SPILL-001) within 24 hours

6.3 Power Failure Recovery

In the event of a power failure:
1. System will automatically shut down (fail-safe)
2. Verify all valves have returned to CLOSED position
3. When power is restored, wait 5 minutes before restarting
4. Follow startup procedure in Section 3.2
5. Perform post-startup verification per Section 3.3

7.0 TROUBLESHOOTING

7.1 Low Flow Rate

Symptom: Flow rate below 15 GPM
Possible causes:
- Clogged filter (check differential pressure across filter)
- Air in the system (check for bubbles in sight glass)
- Pump degradation (check pump RPM and amperage)
- Partially closed valve (verify all valves are fully open)

Resolution:
1. Check and replace filter if differential pressure exceeds 15 PSI
2. Bleed air from system using vent valves at high points
3. If pump performance is degraded, contact Maintenance

7.2 High System Pressure

Symptom: System pressure above 120 PSI
Possible causes:
- Blocked return line
- Failed pressure regulator
- Thermal expansion (temperature too high)

Resolution:
1. Check for obstructions in return piping
2. Verify pressure regulator is functioning
3. If pressure exceeds 150 PSI, initiate emergency shutdown per Section 6.1

7.3 Temperature Control Issues

Symptom: Temperature deviation exceeds +/- 2 degrees F
Possible causes:
- Failed temperature sensor (compare with redundant sensor)
- Controller malfunction (check controller display for error codes)
- Insufficient flow rate (check flow meters)
- Heat exchanger fouling (check approach temperature)

Resolution:
1. Compare primary and redundant sensor readings
2. Reset controller if error code is displayed
3. Verify flow rate is within normal range
4. If heat exchanger fouling is suspected, schedule cleaning

8.0 MAINTENANCE

8.1 Preventive Maintenance Schedule

Daily:
- Visual inspection of all connections and fittings
- Check coolant level in reservoir
- Verify all indicators and alarms are functional

Weekly:
- Inspect coolant for discoloration or contamination
- Check pump seal for leaks
- Test emergency stop buttons

Monthly:
- Replace coolant filters
- Lubricate valve stems
- Check electrical connections for tightness

Annually:
- Full system flush and coolant replacement
- Pump overhaul or replacement
- Heat exchanger inspection and cleaning
- Full electrical system inspection

8.2 Corrective Maintenance

All corrective maintenance actions must be:
1. Authorized by the Shift Supervisor
2. Performed by qualified maintenance personnel
3. Documented in the Maintenance Log (Form TCS-ML-001)
4. Followed by a functional test before returning to service

9.0 DOCUMENTATION AND RECORDS

All records shall be maintained for a minimum of 5 years and include:
- Operations Logs (Form TCS-LOG-001)
- Calibration Records (Form TCS-CAL-001)
- Maintenance Logs (Form TCS-ML-001)
- Incident Reports (Form TCS-IR-001)
- Shift Handover Forms (Form TCS-SHF-001)
